DarkStore - shuttle AS/RS
=========================

Read common.txt first.

1. Overview

Storage is racking with aisles. Each aisle has a rack on both sides, with many
levels. Totes (or trays) sit on shelf locations along the aisle. On each level
a shuttle runs on rails up and down the aisle, and pulls totes out of the rack
or pushes them in. At the front of the aisle one or two lifts move totes
between the levels and the conveyor that feeds the workstations.

So a retrieval is a relay: shuttle to the level buffer, lift down, conveyor to
the station. Coming back it is the same in reverse.

2. Layout

- A location is [aisle][level][side][bay][depth].
  - side is left or right of the aisle
  - bay is the position along the aisle
  - depth is 1 (single deep) or 1..2 (double deep)
- At the front of every level, on each side, a small buffer (transfer
  position) holds totes between the shuttle and the lift. Usually 1 to 3 totes
  per side for outbound and the same for inbound.
- A conveyor loop in front of the aisles (the pre-zone) connects the lift
  outputs with the workstations. Totes can circle the loop when they are early
  or when the station is full.

3. Equipment

Shuttle
  - Runs along one level. Two variants, the simulator should support both:
    - captive: one shuttle per level, it never leaves its level
    - roaming: fewer shuttles than levels, a shuttle changes level by riding
      a lift (a separate shuttle lift, or the tote lift if it can carry one)
  - Telescopic load handler with fingers that reach single or double deep.
    Some handlers hold two totes and can do a swap (put one, take one) in one
    stop.
  - Powered by rail or supercaps. No battery charging trips.
  - Physics: acceleration and braking along the aisle, a fixed time to
    position precisely at the bay, and a load handler cycle per tote.

Lift
  - Moves vertically at the front of the aisle. Carries 1 or 2 totes.
  - Acceleration and braking, plus a transfer time at each end.
  - The lift is usually the bottleneck of an aisle, so model it carefully.

Conveyor
  - Fixed speed, accumulating zones, one tote per zone.

4. Retrieval

1. The IMS picks a tote and reserves a slot in the outbound buffer of its
   level.
2. The shuttle drives to the bay, pulls the tote, and drives to the front.
   It drops the tote in the outbound buffer. If the buffer is full, it waits.
3. The lift collects totes from the buffers and brings them down to the
   conveyor.
4. The conveyor takes the tote to the station queue.

Double deep: if the wanted tote is in the back position and the front
position holds another tote, the shuttle first moves the front tote to a free
location on the same level (a relocation), then fetches the wanted one. This
is the shuttle version of digging, but only ever one tote deep.

The shuttle should combine work where it can: on its way out to fetch a tote,
it carries a returning tote and stores it near the retrieval location.

Sequencing: pick stations often want totes in a set order (for example, all
totes for one order together, or in the order the operator will pick them).
The lift order and the conveyor loop are where the sequence is fixed. A tote
that arrives too early goes around the loop.

5. Traffic

- Captive shuttles never meet each other.
- Roaming shuttles: one shuttle per level at a time. A shuttle can't pass
  another on the same level. Level changes need the shuttle lift, which has to
  be reserved.
- Lifts serve requests from all levels, so they need their own queue and
  dispatch rule (oldest first, or nearest level first, or sequence first).
- When the conveyor or the station queue is full, totes back up into the lift
  and then into the level buffers, and the shuttles wait. Track this as
  blocked time, not idle time.

6. Storage policy

- Spread each SKU over several aisles, so the load is shared and one broken
  aisle doesn't make a SKU unavailable.
- Within an aisle, put fast movers close to the front (short shuttle trip).
  With captive shuttles all levels are equal, so balance by level as well.
- Double deep: put slow movers in the back position, fast movers in front.
- When a tote comes back, store it in the aisle that has the least work queued.

7. Output

On top of the common output:

- Cycles per hour per aisle (in and out), and per lift.
- Utilization of every shuttle and every lift.
- Relocation ratio (double deep): relocation moves divided by retrievals.
- Sequence misses: totes that arrived out of order, and how long they
  circled.
- Time that shuttles were blocked by a full buffer.

8. Parameters

  aisles               4
  levels               20, 0.40 m apart
  aisle length         40 bays, 0.65 m per bay, both sides
  depth                single deep (double deep as an option)
  tote                 600 x 400 mm
  shuttle              4 m/s, accelerate and brake 2 m/s2
  bay positioning      0.5 s
  load handler         3.5 s per pull or push, single deep
                       5 s for the back position
  level buffer         2 totes out, 2 totes in, per side
  lift                 1 or 2 totes, 5 m/s, 7 m/s2, 2 s per transfer
  lifts per aisle      1
  conveyor             0.6 m/s, 0.8 m zones
