DarkStore - case-handling robots (ACR)
======================================

Read common.txt first.

1. Overview

Storage is ordinary shelving with narrow aisles, up to around 10 m high.
Totes or cartons sit on the shelves. Robots drive on the warehouse floor
through the aisles. Each robot has a tall mast with a lifting tote handler,
and a column of shelves on its back where it can carry several totes at
once. It collects a batch of totes on one trip and drops them at a port that
feeds the workstations.

A second variant has robots that climb the racking itself instead of using a
mast. The model below works for both: the only difference is how vertical
movement is timed and whether a robot blocks the aisle while it climbs.

2. Layout

- A location is [row][bay][level][depth], where a row is one side of an
  aisle. Depth is 1 or 2.
- The floor is a grid: aisle cells, cross-aisle cells, port cells, charger
  cells. Aisles are one robot wide.
- Ports sit at the front of the storage area. A port takes totes from the
  robot (one by one, or all at once from the back shelves) and sends them on
  a conveyor to a workstation. Returning totes come back to the port and are
  loaded onto a robot.

3. Equipment

- Robots drive forward and backward and turn on the spot at aisle ends.
- The tote handler moves up and down the mast, turns to face left or right,
  and pulls a tote off the shelf with an arm or clamp. It then puts the tote
  on one of the back shelves.
- The number of back shelves (the onboard buffer) is a setting, usually 5 to
  9.
- The robot only lifts or handles totes while standing still.
- Physics: acceleration and braking, mast speed, handler cycle, battery use
  with charging trips.

4. Retrieval

A trip is a list of stops:

1. The system builds a batch: up to N totes, chosen so that they are close
   together in the racking and are needed soon at the same port.
2. The robot carries returning totes from the port, if there are any. It
   stores them in free locations near the retrieval stops, so it can drop and
   pick in the same aisle.
3. It drives the route (a short travelling-salesman tour through the stops),
   and at each stop it lifts the handler, swaps totes, and lowers it again.
4. At the port it hands over all totes it is carrying, takes on the returning
   ones, and starts the next trip.

Double deep: the same relocation as the shuttle. If the front tote is in the
way, it goes onto a free back shelf of the robot or into a nearby free
location first.

5. Traffic

- One robot per floor cell.
- Aisles are one robot wide, so two robots can't pass each other in an aisle.
  Either make aisles one-way, or lock an aisle (or part of it) for one robot
  at a time. Make this a setting and compare.
- A robot that is handling a tote blocks its aisle cell until the handler is
  done. A climbing robot blocks the rack face it is on.
- Queues form at the ports. A robot waiting at a busy port blocks the cross
  aisle behind it, so give each port a short waiting lane.

6. Storage policy

- Fast movers low (short mast travel) and in the aisles nearest the ports.
- Keep a share of free locations spread over every aisle, so returning totes
  can be stored where the robot is already working.
- Inbound totes are stored the same way: near the next likely pick for that
  SKU, or near the ports if the SKU is fast.

7. Output

On top of the common output:

- Totes per trip, and how full the onboard buffer is on average.
- Trip time split into driving, mast travel, handling, port handover and
  waiting.
- Aisle waits: how often and how long robots waited for another robot in an
  aisle.
- Port utilization and port queue length.
- Relocations (double deep).

8. Parameters

  racking            12 aisles x 30 bays, 1.2 m bays, 20 levels, 0.35 m
                     apart (about 7.5 m high)
  depth              single deep (double deep as an option)
  tote               600 x 400 mm, up to 30 kg
  robots             12
  onboard buffer     8 totes
  drive              2 m/s, accelerate 0.5 m/s2
  mast               0.8 m/s up and down
  handler            8 s to take or place one tote
  ports              3, 1 goods-in, handover 2 s per tote
