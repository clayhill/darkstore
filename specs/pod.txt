DarkStore - pod robots
======================

Read common.txt first. This is the system Kiva made well known: robots that
carry whole shelving units to the operator.

1. Overview

Stock sits on pods: free-standing shelving units on legs, with shelves and
bins on all four sides. The pods stand on the floor in blocks. Small flat
robots drive under a pod, lift it a few centimetres and carry it to a
workstation. The operator picks from the side of the pod that faces them.
Then the robot takes the pod back to a free spot, which doesn't have to be
the spot it came from.

2. Layout

- The floor is a grid of square cells, each about the size of a pod. Every
  cell has a type:
  - storage: a pod may stand here
  - highway: kept free for driving
  - queue: the lane in front of a workstation
  - station: where the pod is presented
  - charger
  - blocked: columns, walls
- Storage cells come in blocks, usually 2 cells deep so every pod touches a
  highway. Highways can be one-way to make traffic simpler.
- A pod has 4 faces. Each face has shelves, each shelf has bins. Stock is
  stored per bin. One SKU is usually spread over many pods.

3. Equipment

- Robots drive forward and backward and rotate on the spot. Unlike the cube
  robot they can turn, but turning takes time.
- While a robot rotates under a lifted pod, it can either keep the pod still
  (a turntable that counter-rotates) or turn the pod with it. Turning the pod
  is how a different face is shown to the operator.
- A robot without a pod is low enough to drive under standing pods. Whether
  it may do that (tunnelling) is a setting. A robot carrying a pod has to stay
  on highways and empty cells.
- Physics: acceleration and braking, rotation speed, lift and lower time,
  heavier pods mean slower acceleration, battery use with charging trips.

4. Retrieval

1. The station has a set of open order lines. The system picks the pod that
   covers the most of them (hit rate), with the travel time as a tie-breaker.
2. A free robot drives to the pod, lifts it, and drives it to the queue of
   that station.
3. At the station the robot turns the pod to the right face. If lines are on
   more than one face, it turns again after the first face is done.
4. When all lines for this pod are picked, the robot takes the pod back to
   storage, or straight to the next station that needs it.

There is no digging: any pod can be reached directly. The cost is that a
whole pod (hundreds of kg) moves for a few picks, so hit rate matters a lot.

5. Traffic

- One robot per cell per time step.
- Plan in space and time: a robot reserves (cell, time) pairs along its route
  before it drives. Space-time A*, or conflict-based search for groups.
  Count rotations as time in the plan.
- Station queues are first in, first out. A robot at the head of the queue
  blocks the lane until the operator is done.
- Watch for deadlocks at highway crossings and in front of busy stations.
  Same remedies as the cube fleet manager: re-route after a timeout, ask idle
  robots to move out of the way.

6. Storage policy

- When a pod goes back, choose the spot by how popular the pod is now: busy
  pods close to the stations, quiet pods further out. Pods move closer over
  time as they get used more, without anyone planning it.
- Inbound: a goods-in station fills bins on a pod. Spread a new SKU over
  several pods so that one pod being busy at a station doesn't block it.
- Optional: re-sort pods in quiet hours.

7. Output

On top of the common output:

- Hit rate: units picked per pod presentation, and lines per presentation.
- Pod presentations per hour per station.
- Average trip time and distance per presentation.
- Queue length in front of each station.
- Congestion map of the floor.
- Empty trips: robot moves without a pod (fetching, returning to charge),
  as a share of all moves.

8. Parameters

  floor               30 x 20 cells, 1.2 m cell
  storage blocks      2 cells deep, 1-cell highways between blocks
  pod                 1 m square, 4 faces, 5 shelves, 3 bins per shelf
                      per face, up to 450 kg loaded
  robots              24
  drive               1.5 m/s, accelerate 1 m/s2 (0.7 m/s2 with a full pod)
  rotate              90 degrees in 1.5 s
  lift / lower        1.5 s each
  tunnelling          on
  stations            4 pick, 1 goods-in, queue of 4 pods each
