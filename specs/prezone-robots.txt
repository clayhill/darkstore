DarkStore - mobile robots between the racking and the stations
===============================================================

Read common.txt first, then shuttle.txt, crane.txt and pallet.txt: this is
a variant of those three types (RailRunner, SkyStack, DeepLane), not a type
of its own. Roadmap item 32. Status: planned, not built.

1. Overview

In RailRunner, SkyStack and DeepLane the totes (or pallets) travel between
the aisle fronts and the stations on the pre-zone conveyor loop. Many real
systems use small mobile robots for that leg instead: the lift or crane
puts the tote on a stand at its aisle front, a robot with a roller top
takes it and drives it to the station's infeed, and on the way back it
brings a finished tote to an aisle.

A setting in each of the three types:

  Transport to the stations: conveyor loop (as now) | mobile robots

The storage, its machines and the stations stay exactly as they are; only
the leg between them changes. Loop against robots on the same store is the
comparison this is for, as SortHub's tilt-tray against its robot sorter:
which needs fewer metres of conveyor or fewer machines, which copes with a
peak, what a breakdown does to each.

2. Layout

The pre-zone loop (js/sim/prezone.js, Racking) is replaced by a robot
floor of whole cells between the aisle fronts and the stations. Today the
aisle fronts are at y = B, the loop's near line at B + 2.9, its far line at
B + 3.8 and the stations' conveyors at D - 1 = B + 4. With robots:

- y = B: a stand per aisle side, at the front of each aisle. The outfeed
  stand (x = 3a, in front of the left rack) holds totes the machines send
  out; the infeed stand (x = 3a + 2, in front of the right rack) holds
  totes coming back. Each holds standCap totes, one behind the other on a
  short conveyor from the I/O point; the robot takes or leaves the front
  one. These cells are blocked for driving.
- y = B + 1: the handover row. A robot stops in front of a stand, facing
  it, to take or leave a tote. Not a through route.
- y = B + 2 and B + 3: two one-way highways, the first running left, the
  second running right (as the loop's lines do now), joined at both ends.
- y = B + 4: the stations' handover row. Each station keeps its conveyor
  infeed and outfeed stubs (Layout.portPath); a robot stops at the end of
  the infeed stub to leave a tote and at the end of the outfeed stub to
  take one. Short queue lanes in front of each station, as on the other
  robot floors (RobotFloor's station lanes).
- Chargers at both ends of the highways, beyond the last aisle and the
  last station.

So D grows by about two rows against the loop. The racking itself, the
I/O point and ioEnd() do not move.

Racking becomes a WS.RobotFloor (js/sim/floor.js) when transport is
robots: the router (js/sim/router.js) works on sim.grid and needs type,
claim, passable(), enterGoal() and canPark(). StackPilot's AcrFloor
(js/acr/sim.js) is the precedent for racking and a robot floor in one
grid. With the loop, Racking stays as it is.

3. Equipment

Roller-top mobile robot (generic, not a vendor's design): a low robot with
a powered roller deck at conveyor height, carrying one tote (in DeepLane
one pallet, on a larger robot: see 10). It drives the grid like the other
floor robots: same Robot class (js/sim/robot.js), speeds, braking and
battery model, its own tasks. Transfer onto or off a stand or a station
stub takes transferTime.

The machines (shuttles, lifts, cranes, carriers) are unchanged. Where they
now check sim.loopRoom() before sending a tote out (shuttle liftOut,
js/shuttle/sim.js; the crane and carrier likewise), they check that the
aisle's outfeed stand has room instead.

4. Retrieval

1. A machine brings the tote to the I/O point as now and puts it on the
   outfeed stand's conveyor (RackSim.convey with a short path; its arrive
   callback holds the tote at the front until a robot takes it).
2. The dispatcher gives the job (stand → station) to a robot: the nearest
   idle one, or one about to finish a drop nearby. A tote is not sent out
   for a station whose queue (in the infeed stub and in robots heading
   there) is full: the same portFull/portRoom rules as on the loop.
3. The robot drives to the handover cell in front of the stand, takes the
   tote, drives along the highway to the station's queue lane and leaves
   the tote on its infeed stub. The station works as now.
4. A tote leaving a station waits on its outfeed stub. The storage policy
   picks its aisle (chooseSlot, unchanged); a robot that has just left a
   tote at that station takes it, if it is free, and drives it to that
   aisle's infeed stand. With the stand full it picks another aisle with
   room (the loop's routeBack rule, at the moment of pickup) or waits.
5. A tote that goes on to another station (stationPath today) goes by
   robot from the outfeed stub straight to the next station's infeed.

Pairing a drop at a station with a pickup there is the main gain of robots
over the loop: no tote goes round empty-handed. The dispatcher should
prefer it, then the oldest waiting tote.

5. Traffic

- One robot per cell, moving-block reservation (the router, unchanged).
- The handover rows are entered only for a handover; robots that wait for
  work park at the ends of the highways or in front of the chargers, not
  on the highways.
- A full infeed stand at an aisle or a full queue lane at a station is the
  robot's equivalent of a full spur: the robot does not stand on the
  highway waiting; it takes another aisle (returning totes) or waits in
  the queue lane's entry (stations, which never send more than they
  can take).
- The loop's late routing (routeConveyors, lap, recirculation) does not
  apply.

6. Storage policy

Unchanged (PreZoneSim.chooseSlot): fast movers in the fastest positions,
spread over the aisles with the least work queued. aisleLoad counts the
totes on robots heading for the aisle instead of those on the loop.

7. Output

On top of the type's own output:
- Robot utilization, waiting, charging (the shared fleet KPIs).
- Empty trips: share of robot trips without a tote.
- Paired trips: share of drops at a station followed by a pickup there.
- Stand waits: time totes wait on an outfeed stand for a robot, and
  machines waiting for room on a full stand (the robots are the limit).
- Distance driven per tote.
The control room's conveyor map becomes a floor map of the robots; the
fleet table lists machines and robots together (both are in sim.fleet).

8. Parameters

  transport            'loop' (default) | 'robots'
  transportRobots      3 per pick station to start with; step 2 tunes it
                       against the loop's lines/h on the default site
  robot speed          1.5 m/s, 0.5 m/s² (the shared robot defaults)
  transferTime         3 s onto or off a stand or a station stub
  standCap             2 totes per stand
  chargers             1 per 6 robots, at the highways' ends
  battery              as the other floor robots

9. Build plan

Each step ends in something that runs, and in a commit. The loop stays the
default, so the golden runs of the existing types must not change in any
step; new golden runs are added for the robot variants.

1. RailRunner with robots, plain: the setting; Racking as a RobotFloor
   with stands, handover rows, highways and chargers when transport is
   robots; roller-top robots and their tasks (take at a stand, leave at a
   station, take at a station, leave at a stand); dispatch without
   pairing; machines checking stand room instead of loop room; a plain 2D
   view of the floor and the robots. Golden run 'shuttle-robots', soak
   invariants (every tote somewhere, no robot on a blocked cell).
2. Dispatch and tuning: pairing drops with pickups, stations going on to
   the next station by robot, parking, charging, the storage policy's
   aisle load; tune transportRobots so the default site picks as many
   lines/h as with the loop, and note where each is the limit.
3. Views: the 2D floor in detail (stands, lanes, highways, chargers), the
   robots in 3D with their roller deck and a tote or pallet on it, the
   control room's floor map, the fleet table, inspectors for stands and
   robots, disruptions (robot breakdowns and charger faults are shared).
4. SkyStack and DeepLane: they share PreZoneSim, so most carries over.
   SkyStack needs only its crane's outfeed check; DeepLane needs the
   larger pallet robot (10). Golden runs 'crane-robots', 'pallet-robots'.
5. README (a paragraph per type and a screenshot pair, loop against
   robots), spec status, roadmap item 32.

10. Open questions to settle before or during step 1

- DeepLane carries pallets: a pallet robot is about 1.2 × 1.0 m, more than
  one cell. Either its floor uses larger cells for the robot leg, or the
  pallet robot claims two cells (the router knows one cell per robot).
  Decide in step 4; if it is costly, the setting comes to RailRunner and
  SkyStack only and DeepLane keeps its loop.
- Racking's x layout (three cells per aisle) gives one stand column per
  rack side; check that two robots can stand at neighbouring stands
  without blocking the highway, or put the handover row's cells only in
  front of the stands.
- The stations' conveyor stubs come from Layout.portPath; check they reach
  the new handover row, or let the stub start one row further down.
- StackPilot's short handover conveyors could get the same option later;
  out of scope here.
