DarkStore - cube storage
========================

Read common.txt first. This is the type the simulator implements today.

1. Overview

The warehouse is a cube storage system, a grid-based AS/RS. Stock is kept in
bins that are stacked directly on top of each other inside an aluminium grid,
with no aisles and no space between the stacks. Robots drive on the top of the
grid and reach the bins from above.

2. Layout

- The grid is a 3D array [X][Y][Z]. X and Y are positions on the top surface,
  Z is the depth of a stack (real systems go up to around 16 bins deep).
- Ports sit on the edge of the grid. Chargers sit on another edge.

3. Equipment

- Small rectangular robots, each one grid cell in size.
- Two sets of wheels at right angles: one set drives along X, the other along Y.
  To change direction the robot stops and lowers the other set (a pneumatic
  shift). It never turns or drives diagonally, only straight X or Y moves.
- A robot always occupies exactly one cell while driving.
- Each robot has a cavity with a winch and a gripper on four bands. It parks
  over a stack, lowers the gripper and latches onto the top bin.
- Physics:
  - acceleration and braking
  - winch speed, slower when it carries a heavy bin than when it is empty
  - battery use, with robots driving to the chargers at the edge of the grid
    on their own when they run low

4. Retrieval (digging)

Only the top bin of a stack can be lifted. If the bin you want is fifth from
the top, the robot first lifts bins 1 to 4 one at a time and sets each one down
on a neighbouring stack. Then it can lift bin 5.

After that the moved bins have to go somewhere. Either a robot puts them back
on the original stack in their original order, or they stay where they are and
the grid slowly re-sorts itself as bins come and go. The simulator should
support both.

Bins are delivered to ports on the edge of the grid. The robot lowers the bin
into the port, and a person (or a picking robot) takes items out or puts them
in.

5. Traffic

- One robot per [X][Y] cell at any time step.
- Robots reserve the cells ahead of them before they drive into them. Path
  planning can use A*, or something like conflict-based search.
- While a robot is digging, its cell can't be passed by anyone until the winch
  is back up.
- The fleet manager re-routes robots when needed, so they don't collide and
  don't end up in gridlock or deadlock.
- State examples: IDLE, MOVING_TO_TARGET, DIGGING_BIN, TRANSPORTING_TO_PORT,
  WAITING_IN_TRAFFIC.

6. Storage policy

Track how popular each product is. Fast movers should be stored near the top
(Z=1) and near the ports. When a fast mover ends up deep in a stack, say at
Z=10 because other bins were put on top of it, log the extra digging time as
a burial penalty.

7. Output

On top of the common output:

- Dig ratio: moves that only shift bins out of the way, divided by moves that
  deliver the bin that was asked for.
- Burial penalties.
- Traffic hotspots on the grid.

8. Parameters

These are the defaults in js/core/config.js.

  grid               26 x 14 cells, 12 bins deep, 0.70 m cell
  fill               80 % of positions hold a bin
  robots             16, 5 chargers
  drive              3.1 m/s, accelerate 0.8 m/s2, brake 1.2 m/s2
  wheel set change   0.7 s
  winch              1.6 m/s empty, 1.1 m/s at the 30 kg maximum payload
  gripper            0.6 s to latch or unlatch
  battery low        go charging below 25 %, stop charging at 92 %
