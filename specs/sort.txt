DarkStore - parcel sorter
=========================

Read common.txt first, but note: a sorter has no stock and no orders. Of
common.txt only the time model, the control room and the barcodes apply.

1. Overview

A parcel hub. Parcels arrive in a stream (from trucks, or from the packing
stations of a fulfilment centre), each labelled for one destination: a
route, a postcode area, a carrier depot. Operators at induction stations
scan each parcel and put it on the sorter, which drops it into the chute
for its destination. Every chute ends in a bag or cage; people change full
bags. Labels the scanner cannot read go to a reject chute, where someone
handles them by hand.

Two kinds of sorter, compared on the same parcel stream:
- Tilt-tray loop: trays on a chain run round a loop past the chutes; at the
  right chute a tray tilts and the parcel slides off.
- Robot sorter: small robots on a raised deck each carry one parcel from an
  induct to a hole above its chute's bag and tip it in.

2. Layout

- Destinations: a list with a share of the volume each; a few take most of
  it (falling off with rank).
- Chutes: one per destination, a second (third, ...) for the busiest ones,
  and the last one for rejects.
- Tilt-tray: the loop is two straights joined by half circles; chutes on the
  outside of both straights, the inducts at the start of the top one, fed
  by a belt from the inbound dock.
- Robot sorter: a deck of cells with a hole every third cell in both
  directions, the induct lanes along the front, chargers and parking along
  the back. Bags hang under the holes.

3. Equipment

- Induct: an operator takes a parcel off the feed belt and scans it (2.4 s),
  scans the next while the sorter takes the last one.
- Tilt-tray: trays 1 m apart at 2 m/s. An induct loads the next empty tray.
- Robots: about 0.6 m square, 2 m/s, quick to accelerate and turn, a tilt
  tray on top (0.8 s to tip), battery with charging.
- Bag workers walk to a full bag and change it (25 s).

4. Sorting

Tilt-tray: a tray passing one of its parcel's chutes tips it in if the bag
has room. Otherwise the parcel goes round again (recirculation); after
three laps it goes to the reject chute.

Robot sorter: a robot queues at an induct, takes the parcel, and drives to
the nearest chute of its destination with room. With every one of them
full the parcel goes to the overflow (reject) chute. A chute that fills up
while the robot is on its way is chosen again on arrival; with nowhere to
go at all the robot waits in the parking row.

5. Traffic (robot sorter)

- One-way streets between the holes: of the two rows between hole rows one
  runs east and one west, of the two columns between hole columns one runs
  south and one north.
- No stopping inside a crossing: a robot only drives into one when it can
  drive on out of it.
- Inducts are pass-throughs: robots come down a southbound street into the
  lane and leave straight on onto a northbound one. No more robots head for
  an induct than its lane holds and a few on their way; robots with nothing
  to do park along the back.

6. Policy

Arriving parcels go to the induct with the shortest feed. A destination's
parcels go to its chute nearest the robot (robot sorter) or the first of
its chutes the tray passes with room (tilt-tray).

7. Output

- Sort rate and induction rate per hour, parcels waiting to be inducted.
- Recirculation (tilt-tray) or overflow (robots), rejects and no-reads.
- Bags changed, chutes full now, bag workers' utilization.
- Tray occupancy, or the robots' time split and battery.

8. Parameters

- 3600 parcels/h, 28 destinations, 36 chutes, 4 inducts, 2 % no-reads.
- Bags of 30 parcels, 2 bag workers, 25 s per change.
- Tilt-tray: 2 m/s, trays 1 m apart, 3 laps before reject.
- Robots: 45, 2 m/s, 2 m/s² acceleration, 0.35 s to turn, 10 chargers.
