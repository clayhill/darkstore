DarkStore - mixed-case pallet DC
================================

Read common.txt first, but note: this type keeps its own stock (cases of
grocery and household goods), its own orders (stores) and does not use the
shared stations or packing. Of common.txt the time model, the control room
and the disruptions apply. Implemented as CaseCraft (casecraft.html,
js/case/).

1. Overview

A distribution centre that supplies supermarkets. Suppliers deliver
single-product pallets; stores order a few hundred cases of many products
and want them on mixed pallets they can put straight into their aisles.
Everything in between is automated, case by case:

- Depalletising: a robot arm takes the cases off an incoming pallet one by
  one onto a conveyor. A tunnel measures and weighs each case.
- Storage: lifts take the cases up into a dense structure of many levels.
  On every level, case bots drive along a transfer deck and into narrow
  aisles, and set each case on a shelf in a gap just wide enough for it.
- Sequencing: for each pallet to build, the bots bring its cases to an
  output lift, and the lift takes them down in the exact order the pallet
  is built in.
- Palletising: a robot arm stacks the cases into a mixed pallet, heavy at
  the bottom and crushable on top, in a stable stack planned beforehand.
  The pallet is stretch-wrapped, labelled and loaded into the store's
  truck.

Inspired by case-handling distribution systems in general (Symbotic,
Witron, Swisslog, Dematic and others); not any one product.

2. Layout

Positions in metres: x east, y south, z up.

- The structure: A aisles (default 16) running north-south, 12 m long, 0.8
  m wide, with shelves 0.65 m deep on both sides (back to back between
  aisles), on L levels (default 14, 0.72 m apart, the first 0.9 m up).
  The first 0.8 m of an aisle has no shelf, so a bot working there stays
  clear of the deck.
  Each level is a floor of its own: a transfer deck along the south end
  joins its aisles to the lifts. A shelf is a continuous plank: a case
  takes as much of it as it is wide, plus 3 cm.
- The deck has two lanes 1.25 m apart: the north one (by the aisle
  mouths) westbound, the south one eastbound. On its south side, for
  every lift, a transfer shelf of three places, and round the middle a
  parking pocket for every bot of the level.
- Lifts: input lifts (default 3) west of the parking pockets, output
  lifts east of them, one for each palletising cell (default 3).
- South of the west half: the inbound door in the west wall, a pallet
  conveyor past the depalletising cells (default 3, each with a place for
  the pallet it works on and one for the next), the case conveyor through
  the measuring tunnel, and a spur to each input lift.
- South of the east half: the palletising cells (one per output lift), a
  stretch wrapper and labeller, and a conveyor round to the doors in the
  south wall, one below each cell, with a store's truck backed up to it.

3. Equipment

- Case bot: 1.2 x 0.62 m, 6 m/s on the deck, 5 m/s in an aisle, 4 m/s²;
  a quarter turn takes 0.6 s; putting a case on or taking it off a shelf
  2.2 s (its arms reach under the case from the side). Bots never leave
  their level. Default 3 per level.
- Lift: 5 m/s, 5 m/s², a carriage for 3 cases; 0.9 s to take a case from
  or put one on a transfer shelf place, 0.8 s to load one at the bottom,
  0.6 s to put one on the conveyor at the bottom.
- Depalletising arm: 2.6 s a case. Pallets arrive by truck (22 pallets) and
  a forklift puts them on the pallet conveyor; an emptied pallet goes on a
  stack of empties.
- Palletising arm: 3.4 s a case. A new pallet takes 8 s to come in.
- Stretch wrapper 35 s, labeller 6 s, pallet conveyors 0.4 m/s, case
  conveyors 0.8 m/s.

4. Flow

Inbound. Each product has a reorder point (coverHours of its demand, at
least 6 cases); below it, a pallet is ordered for the next inbound truck
(at most every 30 minutes, if there is something to bring), as long as
the structure has room for it. A truck brings the most urgent first: the
products orders wait for, then those with the least cover. The forklift puts the
pallets on the conveyor. A pallet turns into a depalletising cell only
when that cell will be ready for it within 20 s (its place for the next
pallet free, the cases left at the pace the arm has managed the last few
minutes, a full outfeed included); otherwise it goes on to the next cell,
or waits on the conveyor in front of this one if a pallet already waits
in front of the next. So no pallet is promised early to a busy cell. A
pallet that waits in a cell whose arm breaks down, or that a cell further
along would start sooner, rolls back onto the conveyor and goes on (the
conveyor runs one way: not back to an earlier cell); the cell's
inspector can send it on by hand. The outfeeds join the case conveyor as
a zip merge: a case coming along waits for one taken off earlier that
waits to join, so the cell nearest the tunnel is not starved of gaps.

Storage. As a case passes the tunnel it gets its place: the level with the
least to do among those holding the fewest cases of its product (so a
pallet needing ten of them gets them from several levels at once), then
the aisle and gap by the storage policy, the emptier shelves first so
the aisles fill alike. The place is reserved at once.
The case rides to the input lift with the shortest queue; the lift takes up
to three cases, puts each on its level's transfer shelf, and a bot of that
level takes it to its place.

Orders. Store orders arrive (default 9 an hour, about 250 cases each, a
Poisson stream). Each order is planned into pallets at once (section 5); its
pallets queue for the palletising cells.

Sequencing. A cell starting a pallet releases its cases in build order:
for each, the stored case of that product nearest the deck is taken, and a
place on the output lift's transfer shelf of that case's level is reserved.
Places on one level are reserved in build order, at most three at a time,
so the case the lift needs next always has one. The lift collects the next
cases in order (up to three, as far as they are already waiting), takes
them down and puts them on the conveyor to its cell.

Bots. A free bot takes, in this order: a case its output lift needs within
the next six; a case on its level's input shelf; any other case to fetch.
After putting a case away it fetches one from the same aisle if there is
one (a dual cycle). A bot needs the aisle (or the lift's shelf) it goes
to to itself, and enters the deck only once it has reserved its lane from
here to there, and the other lane where it crosses it; the lane behind
it is free again as it drives on. A bot whose destination is taken waits
in its own parking pocket, so nothing locks up.

Outbound. The arm puts each case on its planned place. The finished
pallet is wrapped, labelled and conveyed to the store's door; when all of
an order's pallets are on its truck, the truck leaves and the next store's
truck backs up.

5. The pallet planner

A pallet is 1.2 x 1.0 m, loaded up to 1.6 m high and 800 kg. The planner
takes the order's cases in the policy's order and gives each a place:

- Candidates are the corners next to cases already placed, both ways
  round. A case goes where it ends up lowest, then furthest back and left.
- It is turned either way round, whichever leaves no strip too narrow
  for another like it.
- It must stand on at least 75 % of its base, on cases strong enough to
  carry it: fragile cases (crisps, paper) carry up to 5 kg; normal ones
  up to 14 kg.
- Two pallets are open at a time: a case goes on the first it fits on;
  else a new one is opened and the older of the two closed.

Build order (the policy):
- Stable: strongest first, then heaviest, then tallest: a store's aisles
  mixed over all its pallets.
- Store aisle: by the store's aisle (walk order), heavy first within each:
  each pallet goes to fewer aisles, but crushables end up low and heavy
  cases cannot go on them.
- Blend (default): heavy, normal and light cases in three bands, each in
  aisle order.

6. Policies

- Storage: best fit (the gap the case fills best, any gap more than 60 cm
  too big counting as open shelf; fast movers near the deck) or nearest
  the deck.
- Build order: stable, store aisle, blend.
- Bots per level, lifts, cells.

7. Output

- Cases in (depalletised) and out (palletised) per hour, pallets per hour,
  store orders shipped.
- Pallet fill (share of 1.2 x 1.0 x 1.6 m used), cases and aisles per
  pallet, height and weight.
- Structure: cases stored, shelf length used, products below their
  reorder point.
- Bots, lifts and arms busy; cells waiting for their next case.

With the defaults the system keeps up with about 2,200 cases an hour in
and out: three palletising cells at about 740 cases an hour each, store
pallets of about 43 cases, 1.4 m high and about 55 % full; an order is on
its truck 40 to 60 minutes after it came in. The output lifts are the
busiest machines (about 90 %).

Not modelled: layer picking, rejects at the tunnel, mixed-temperature
goods, slip sheets and tier sheets, bots changing level.
