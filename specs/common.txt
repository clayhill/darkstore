DarkStore - shared simulation rules
===================================

Everything in this file applies to every storage type, unless a type spec
says otherwise.

1. Time and state

- Fixed time steps of 100 ms, plus an event queue for things that happen at a
  known time. The steps need to be small enough to show travel, lifting and
  picking times properly.
- Every machine (robot, shuttle, lift, crane) runs its own state machine. The
  state names differ per type, but each one should map onto the common
  buckets used in the output: working, waiting, idle, charging, fault.
- A run with the same seed and the same settings gives the same result.
- The renderer only reads the state and interpolates between steps. It never
  changes the simulation.

2. Words used in all specs

  storage unit   the thing that is moved to a workstation. A bin in cube
                 storage, a tote or tray in a shuttle system, a pod (shelf)
                 for pod robots, a tote or carton for ACR, a tote or pallet
                 for a crane.
  location       the place where a storage unit sits when it is stored.
  workstation    where a person (or a picking robot) takes items out of a
                 storage unit or puts them in. Pick stations and goods-in
                 stations.
  presentation   one storage unit arriving at a workstation and being worked.

3. Parts that every type shares

Order management
  - Orders stream in without waves, from a Poisson process or entered by hand.
    An order has one or more lines (SKU, quantity).
  - Empty order totes stand nested on a dolly at every pick station; the
    totes are shared out over the stations at the start.
  - An order is released to the least loaded pick station that has a free
    put-wall slot and an empty tote on its stack. The operator takes the
    tote off the stack and puts it in the slot (2.5 s); no line is picked
    into it before it is there.
  - The operator picks to light from the storage unit into the order tote.
    A complete tote goes by conveyor to packing, gets an SSCC label, and goes
    to the dock. The truck leaves when it is full or at the cut-off time.
  - The emptied tote goes back from packing (60 s) to the station with the
    smallest stack.
  - Each station's stack light sits on top of the operator's screen, clear of
    the conveyors.
  - Machines' status lights fade to the colour of a new state, and breathe
    slowly while a machine idles, waits or charges.
  - Each operator's screen shows the live job: the line being picked or
    decanted (quantity, product, the compartment it comes from or goes into,
    the put-wall slot, the order and customer), the job's progress, or what
    the station is waiting for. Pack benches show the order, its address and
    carrier.

Inventory (IMS)
  - Item master, storage units with 1, 2 or 4 compartments (pods have shelves
    and faces instead, see pod.txt), stock per compartment.
  - Stock is reserved per order line. The IMS chooses which storage unit to
    fetch. When several units hold the SKU, prefer one that is already on its
    way to that station, then the one that is cheapest to fetch.
  - Pick velocity per SKU, decaying over time, and ABC classes (top 20 %,
    next 30 %, rest). Each type uses this in its own storage policy.
  - Inbound works from an ASN: the IMS reserves compartment space, filling
    same-SKU compartments first, and the storage units go to a goods-in
    station to be filled. Optional min/max replenishment creates ASNs on its
    own.

Workstations
  - A station has a small queue of storage units waiting in front of it. The
    operator time per pick and per put is a setting.
  - A storage unit that is needed at more than one station goes straight from
    one to the next before it is stored again.

Barcodes
  - GS1: GTIN on items, GRAI on storage units and totes, SSCC on shipping
    labels, all under the demo company prefix 9521234.

Energy
  - Battery machines drain by activity and go to a charger on their own when
    they get low. Machines on power rails (most shuttles, all cranes) skip
    this.

4. Output

Every type reports at least:

- Throughput: storage units presented per hour, and order lines picked per
  hour (LPH).
- Order cycle time, from release to packed.
- Machine utilization, split into working, waiting, idle, charging and fault.
- Workstation utilization, and how long stations sit starved (nothing to
  work on) or blocked (nowhere to send the unit).
- Bottleneck alerts: busy areas, starved stations, backorders, tote
  shortage, low battery.

5. Comparing types

The point of having several types is comparing them. So the same order
profile, SKU set and number of workstations should run on any type. For the
comparison, every type also reports:

- storage density: storage units (and units of stock) per m2 of floor
- units presented per hour per machine
- the share of moves that do not deliver anything (digging in a cube,
  relocations in double-deep racking, pod returns, empty trips)
