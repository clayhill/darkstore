DarkStore - automated car park
==============================

Read common.txt first, but note: a car park has no stock and no orders. Of
common.txt only the time model, the control room and the disruptions apply.
Implemented as RoboValet (robovalet.html, js/park/).

1. Overview

A multi-storey car park without ramps, people or lighting on its parking
levels. A driver drives into an entry/exit cabin (EET) on the street,
gets out, confirms at a terminal and walks away. Machines take the car up
to a bay and bring it back when the driver returns, turned round so it can
be driven out forwards.

The machines are those of an AGV car park:
- Parking robots: flat, omnidirectional AGVs (about 3.9 x 1.1 x 0.12 m)
  that drive under a car, swing out clamp arms round its four tyres and
  lift it a few centimetres. They move sideways as easily as forwards and
  turn a car on the spot.
- Car lifts: a platform per shaft that carries one car between the ground
  floor and the parking levels.

Because nobody walks on the parking levels, they are low (2.4 m floor to
floor instead of about 3 m) and need no ramps, walkways or lights: more
cars fit in the same volume. The price is that every car passes through a
cabin and a lift, and these set the throughput.

2. Layout

- Ground floor (3.6 m high): along the street a row of modules, each a
  cabin (4 m wide) and, at the back of the building, a car lift. Between
  them a transfer zone where a ground robot carries cars straight across.
  Beside the modules the pedestrian lobby (entrance, pay kiosks, seats, a
  screen with the cars on their way down), and a plant room.
- Parking levels (default 5): one aisle 6 m wide along the building, bays
  of 2.6 x 5.4 m at right angles to it on both sides. The lift shafts take
  the first bays of the north row.
- One robot per parking level, one ground robot per module. Robots never
  leave their floor and the lifts carry only the car, so two robots never
  meet.
- In front the street: a lane each way. Arriving cars queue in the near
  lane; leaving cars pull out across into the far lane.

Defaults: 4 modules, 5 levels, 16 bays in the north row after the shafts,
22 in the south row: 190 bays.

3. Equipment

- Robot: 2.0 m/s empty, 1.2 m/s with a car, 0.5 m/s^2; turns 30 deg/s.
  Clamping and lifting a car 6 s, setting it down 5 s.
- Lift: 1.0 m/s, 0.5 m/s^2; 2 s to level with a floor and open the gate,
  1.2 s to close it.
- Cabin: a car drives in at walking pace; the driver's handover takes 40 s
  (get out, confirm at the terminal, leave); the door closes and the car is
  measured and checked (8 s). Getting in again takes 15 s.
- People walk at 1.3 m/s; paying at the kiosk takes 20 s.

Published systems of this kind store or retrieve a car in about two
minutes; one cabin handles about 30 cars an hour.

4. Storing and retrieving

Store: when the car in a cabin is checked and the module's lift is free,
the lift is booked for it and a bay is chosen (policy, below) and
reserved. The ground robot drives under the car, clamps it, carries it
back into the transfer zone (the cabin is free again), waits for the lift
at the ground floor, puts the car on the platform and backs out. The lift
goes up; that level's robot drives in, takes the car, carries it sideways
along the aisle and into its bay, and sets it down.

Retrieve: the driver comes back, pays at a kiosk and sits down, or ordered
the car in an app a few minutes earlier. The oldest request is served by a
free cabin whose lift is free too, the lift nearest the car's level first;
the cabin is held for the car from then on. The level robot fetches the car
from its bay, turns it round on the way to the lift (so it comes down
facing the street) and puts it on the platform; the lift comes down; the
ground robot carries it into the cabin. The driver walks over, gets in and
drives off.

A free cabin takes either the head of the street queue or the oldest
request, whichever has waited longer.

5. Traffic

There is no traffic between robots (one per floor). What can wait:
- A lift takes one job at a time and is booked only when it is free, so a
  robot only ever waits for a lift that is already on its way to it. This
  is what keeps the system free of deadlocks; the cost is that a cabin is
  held empty while its car comes down.
- A level robot works its jobs one after the other, a lift already
  waiting at its floor first.
- Drivers queue in the street; past maxQueue cars, and when no bay is free
  for them (counting the cars still on their way in), they go elsewhere.

6. Storage policy

- Balanced (default): the free bay that is quickest to reach from the
  module's lift, plus a penalty for each job the level's robot still has:
  stores spread over the levels in a rush.
- Nearest: the quickest bay to reach; low levels near the lifts fill
  first.
- By stay: the driver tells the terminal when they will be back. Free bays
  are sorted by how quick they are to reach, and a car takes the one at the
  share of its stay in a 9-hour day: short stays near the lifts and low,
  all-day cars far along and high up.

7. Output

- Occupancy, cars parked and delivered per hour.
- Driver wait (from arriving in the lobby to the car ready in a cabin):
  average and 90th percentile over the last hour; retrieval time (request
  to car ready); drivers waiting now.
- Street queue, drivers turned away (and how many because the car park
  was full).
- Cabins, lifts and robots busy over the last 15 minutes; share of the
  cabins held for cars on their way down.

8. Parameters (the day)

The simulation starts at 06:00.
- Residents: 40 cars parked overnight. 60 % leave between 06:30 and 10:00
  (around 07:45) and come back around 18:30; 20 % go out for one to three
  hours during the day; 20 % stay.
- Commuters: 120, arriving around 08:15 (standard deviation 27 min) and
  leaving around 17:15 (45 min), at least 4 hours later.
- Visitors: 14 an hour from 10:00 to 20:00, staying 30 min to 4 h.
- 30 % of drivers order the car in the app 4 minutes ahead.
- A rush (Disruptions) brings visitors 2.5 times as fast.

With the defaults the morning rush makes a short queue in the street; in
the evening rush drivers wait about 4 to 5 minutes on average, the slowest
tenth about 8 minutes, with the cabins nearly always busy and about three
of four held for cars on their way down: the cabins, not the robots, are
the bottleneck.

Not modelled: robot batteries and charging, cars too large for the bays
or the scanner rejecting a car, re-parking cars overnight ahead of the
morning, electric car charging bays.
