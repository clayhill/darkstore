DarkStore - pharmacy wholesale: A-frame and zone picking
========================================================

Read common.txt first. Implemented as ZoneHop.

1. Overview

The system a pharmacy wholesaler uses to deliver many small orders to
pharmacies several times a day. It turns the other types round: no storage
unit travels to a picker. The order tote travels past the stock instead,
zone after zone, until the order is complete, and then through a row of
machines that close it and send it to the right van.

  order start  →  A-frame  →  zones (pick-to-light, carousel, RF)  →
  merge  →  check weight  →  documents  →  lid  →  label  →
  shipping sorter  →  route lanes  →  vans

Products are small boxes (medicines, about 0.05-0.5 L), so one order tote
holds a typical pharmacy order of 10 to 40 lines. Totes come back from the
pharmacies, are emptied and stacked, and go round again.

2. Layout

Everything is conveyor; there is no robot floor. In the model:

- The main line runs from order start past the A-frame to a loop with a
  spur into every picking zone, then to the merge and the closing line.
- A-frame: one frame with channels on both sides, a belt running underneath
  its full length. Channel k is a location; it holds one SKU.
- Pick-to-light zones: flow racks along a spur, a light per location. A tote
  turns into the spur only if its order has lines there (zone skipping) and
  waits at the zone's pick position.
- Carousel zone: horizontal carousels (a ring of shelves turning past one
  operator), a light per shelf position.
- RF zone: ordinary shelving for slow movers; a picker walks it with an RF
  scanner and picks into a separate tote, which joins its order at the merge.
- Closing line: weighing scale, document printer and inserter, lidding
  machine, label printer and applicator.
- Shipping sorter: a line with a diverter per route lane; each lane is the
  buffer for one van.
- Tote return: totes back from the vans' previous trips are tipped empty by
  the tote-emptying machine, stacked, and fed to order start by the
  de-stacker.

3. Equipment

A-frame
  - Each order gets its own stretch of belt (a window). As its window passes
    a channel holding one of its products, the dispenser ejects the units
    (a fixed time per unit; a channel ejects one unit at a time).
  - At the end of the belt the window tips into the order tote waiting
    there. The belt only moves on when that tote is in place.
  - Channels empty as they dispense. An operator behind the frame refills
    them from bins taken from a flow rack behind the walkway, which a
    shuttle store keeps supplied (a fixed time per refill, units per refill).
    A channel below its refill level raises a refill request; refill
    operators work the most urgent first. A line whose channel is empty when
    its window passes is short: the tote is sent to the completion zone
    (pick-to-light) for the missing units.
Pick-to-light zone
  - One operator per zone. The tote stops at the pick position, the lights
    of its lines turn on with the quantity, the operator picks and confirms
    each (time per line plus per unit), then the tote moves on.
  - Up to a few totes queue on the spur; when it is full, a tote stays on
    the loop and goes round once more (as in RailRunner).
Carousel zone
  - The carousel turns to the shelf with the next line (travel time from
    the distance round the ring), the operator picks and confirms.
  - Two carousels per operator, so one turns while the operator picks from
    the other.
RF zone
  - A picker takes a batch of orders' RF lines, walks a route through the
    shelving (walking speed, time per line), picks into one tote per order
    and puts the totes onto the merge conveyor.
Conveyors
  - Fixed speed; totes keep their distance and give way where lines meet
    (the conveyor model of the shared order totes).
Closing line
  - Each machine works one tote at a time, with a fixed cycle: scale,
    document inserter, lidding machine, labeller. A tote that fails the
    weight check (an error rate) goes to a manual check station.
Shipping sorter and vans
  - Each tote is diverted into its route's lane. A van has fixed departure
    times; at departure it takes what is in its lane, up to its capacity.
    Totes that miss it wait for the next trip.

4. Order flow

1. Pharmacies order through the day, with peaks before each van departure;
   an order belongs to the route of its pharmacy. Lines are split by zone
   from the product's slotting.
2. Order start: the de-stacker feeds an empty tote, a label printer gives it
   the order's code. An order too large for one tote gets two.
3. The A-frame window is filled while the tote waits at the end of the belt
   and then tipped into it.
4. The tote turns into every zone its order needs, and only those.
5. At the merge, the order's RF tote (if any) joins it; the order waits
   there until all its totes are in.
6. Closing line, shipping sorter, van.

5. Traffic

- A full zone spur: totes for it go round the loop again.
- A full merge buffer or closing line backs up into the loop: order start
  stops releasing totes while the loop is three quarters full.
- The A-frame belt waits for its tote at the end; the de-stacker waits for
  free belt windows.

6. Slotting and replenishment

- Products are ranked by units per day. The fastest that fit the A-frame's
  channels (small, regular boxes) go there; the next ones to pick-to-light,
  then the carousels; the long tail to the RF shelving.
- A-frame refill: refill level and refill quantity per channel; refill
  operators are the A-frame's limit at peak.
- Zones are replenished from a reserve store (not modelled: stock in the
  zones is topped up after a fixed delay).

7. Output

On top of the common output:

- Order totes out per hour, lines per hour per zone, the A-frame's share of
  units.
- Zone utilization and zone skipping (share of totes that skip each zone).
- A-frame: empty channels now, refill requests waiting, short lines sent to
  completion.
- Loop recirculation, merge wait time.
- Totes per van and per route, totes that missed their van.
- Control room map: the conveyor network with totes, zone and machine
  states, and the route lanes.

8. Parameters

Tuned once the model ran (the first guesses in brackets): the first
guesses overloaded the pick-to-light zones, and the vans and totes could
not carry 400 orders an hour.

  products             1200 SKUs; A-frame 240 channels (120 per side),
                       pick-to-light 3 zones of 100 locations (2 of 150),
                       carousel zone 2 carousels of 60 shelves, RF 600
                       locations
  orders               250 per hour on average (400), 8-30 lines (10-40),
                       1-3 units per line; waves up to 1.6 times the
                       average just before a tour leaves
  A-frame              belt 0.5 m/s, window 1.2 m, 0.25 s per unit,
                       channels 80 units, refill at 20, 30 s per refill,
                       2 refill operators
  pick-to-light        2 s per line plus 1.2 s per unit (1.5), 4 totes
                       per spur
  carousel             0.5 m/s ring speed, 3 s per line plus 0.8 s per unit
  RF                   3 pickers, 1.1 m/s walking, 8 s per line, batches
                       of 8 orders
  merge                6 s to tip an RF tote into its order tote
  conveyors            0.6 m/s, a tote and its gap 0.7 m
  closing line         scale 2 s, documents 4 s, lid 3 s, label 3 s,
                       weight errors 1 %, manual check 40 s
  routes               12 routes (8), vans of 60 totes; a tour every 2 h
                       (3 a day), its vans 90 s apart; a van is back with
                       the empty totes after 90 min; cut-off 20 min
  totes                1000 in circulation (300)

9. Build plan

Each step ends in something that runs, and in a commit.

1. Simulation core and a plain 2D view: orders with routes, order start,
   the conveyor loop with pick-to-light zones, merge, closing line as one
   machine, shipping lanes and vans. Golden test.
2. The A-frame: windows, dispensing, tipping into the tote, channels
   emptying and refill operators, short lines to completion.
3. Carousel and RF zones, the merge of RF totes, the full closing line.
4. The 2D view in detail, the control room (conveyor map, zone cards,
   route lanes), KPIs and the inspectors.
5. The 3D view, with the A-frame as its centrepiece; Follow an order.
6. Landing page card and preview, README section, soak test invariants,
   disruptions (an A-frame refill operator on break, a jammed channel).

10. Decisions

- Name: ZoneHop.
- All four zones (A-frame, pick-to-light, carousels, RF) in the first
  release, built in the steps above.
- Orders arrive in waves before the van departures; totes that miss their
  van wait for the next trip.
- The closing line and route vans replace the shared packing benches and
  truck.
- Made while building: steps 1 to 3 were built together. The RF tote is
  tipped into its order tote at the merge by an operator (a separate tote
  only when it does not fit), so most orders ship as one tote. Pick-to-
  light products are shared out over the zones by demand, so each zone has
  about as many lines. The first pick-to-light zone is the completion
  zone, picking short A-frame lines from its reserve. A breakdown jams one
  of the busiest A-frame channels. The robot cam rides an order tote.
- Made after the first release: order start is past the end of the
  A-frame, not at its start: the tote's first stop is the hopper under the
  end of the A-frame's belt, where it waits only when its order has
  A-frame lines, then a feed conveyor climbs to the loop. The A-frame is
  replenished as at Symbion Brisbane (SSI Schaefer): behind each walkway a
  flow rack holds bins of every channel's product (2 per lane); the refill
  operator takes one, sets it on the ledge at the foot of the A-frame,
  fills the channel and puts the empty bin on the rack's return lane. A
  Cuby shuttle store (3 aisles, 10 levels with a shuttle each, a lift per
  aisle) sends a new bin for each one taken, over a conveyor to the back
  of the lane (25 s to the lift, one every 8 s per lift), and takes the
  empty bins back; its own stock is not modelled. The carousels are drawn
  as carriers of six shelves with a bin on each, hanging from an overhead
  track, one product per carrier. The merge is drawn as a put-to-light
  station: a rack of tote lanes (three levels) with a light under each,
  the merge position on the line in front of the operator, the RF totes'
  accumulation lane behind them and a dolly for the empty RF totes.
