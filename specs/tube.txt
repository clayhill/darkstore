DarkStore - a hospital's pneumatic tube system and its pharmacy
================================================================

Read common.txt first, but note: this type keeps its own stations,
carriers and medicine stock and does not use the shared stock, stations,
orders or packing. Of common.txt the time model, the control room and the
disruptions apply. Not a warehouse. Implemented as AirPost (airpost.html,
js/tube/).

1. Overview

A hospital moves small things all day: blood and other samples from the
wards to the lab, medicines from the pharmacy to the wards, units of blood
from the blood bank to theatres. A pneumatic tube system does it in
carriers, plastic capsules about 40 cm long that air blows through a
network of tubes.

- Stations: on every ward, in the emergency department, theatres, ICU,
  outpatients and radiology, and at the pharmacy and the lab. A station
  has a send dock, a basket carriers land in, and a rack of empty
  carriers.
- Lines (zones): one tube with one blower. A line moves one carrier at a
  time, from station to station through its diverters. Each wing has a
  riser up through its floors; the pharmacy with the carrier store have
  the core line, the lab a line of its own.
- The transfer unit: where the lines meet, in the plant room. A carrier
  for another line lands in it, is shifted across and waits for that
  line.
- The pharmacy: a dispensing robot picks the packs of a medicine order
  from its shelves; the pharmacist checks them and sends them up.
- Empty carriers: nothing can be sent without one. They pile up at the lab
  and run short on the wards.

Inspired by hospital tube systems in general (Swisslog TransLogic,
Aerocom, Sumetzberger and others) and by pharmacy picking robots (BD Rowa,
Omnicell and others); not any one product.

2. Layout

Positions in metres: x east along the building, y south (to the street),
z up.

- Wings (default 3) side by side along the street, 24 m wide and 18 m
  deep; a ground floor and floors above it (default 5), 4 m apart.
- On every floor a corridor along the building with rooms on both sides.
  The ground floor: the emergency department in the west wing, the
  pharmacy, plant room and lab in the middle, outpatients (or radiology)
  in the east. The first floor: theatres in the west, ICU in the middle.
  Wards above.
- A station hangs on the corridor wall of each department. A wing's
  riser stands in a shaft room beside its corridor; at each floor a
  diverter sends the branch along the ceiling to that floor's station.
- The transfer unit in the plant room, the carrier store beside it, the
  core line's and the lab line's blowers there too; the risers' blowers
  on the roof.

3. Equipment

- Tubes: carriers fly at 6 m/s (2 m/s² up and down), samples and blood at
  3 m/s. Before a leg the diverters are set and the blower starts (3 s);
  at the end the carrier slows on an air cushion and drops into the
  basket (2 s).
- Transfer unit: 5 s to shift a carrier across; it holds 4.
- Carriers: 90 in the system, each with a home station.
- People: 20 s to load a carrier, 15 s to empty one; a ward notices a
  carrier has landed after about 150 s on average (an urgent one beeps:
  a third of that), the lab after about 20 s.
- Dispensing robot: a mast on rails in an aisle between two walls of
  shelves, 11 shelves high, about 140 products. 1.2 m/s along the aisle,
  0.9 m/s up and down; 2.5 s to grab a pack, 1.5 s to drop it in the
  chute. The pharmacist checks an order in 25 s.

4. Flow

Samples: a ward sends about 3 an hour (twice as many on the morning round,
06:30 to 09:00), the ED 12, ICU 8; some are urgent (the ED half of them).
A nurse puts the sample in an empty carrier, puts it in the send dock and
sets the lab as its destination.

Medicines: a ward orders about 1.5 times an hour, 1 to 4 lines. The robot
picks the most urgent order first, line by line; the pharmacist checks it
and sends it to the ward in a carrier. Wholesaler deliveries five times a
day refill what has fallen below its minimum; the robot puts the packs
away between orders.

Blood: the lab's blood bank sends about 2 units an hour to theatres, ICU
or the ED, always urgent.

A carrier for a station on its own line goes there in one leg; one for
another line goes to the transfer unit first, then on that line. Where it
lands someone empties it, and it stays there as an empty carrier.

5. Traffic

A line moves one carrier at a time and is held for the whole leg:
switching, the flight and the landing. A line takes, of the carriers
waiting in its stations' send docks and those shifted to it in the
transfer unit, an urgent one first (unless urgent ones wait their turn),
empties last, else the one that has waited longest. A leg starts only
when its basket (or the transfer unit) has room. Carriers queue in a send
dock behind the one in front.

6. Policies

- Empty carriers: 'store' (a station below its par of empties, 3, gets
  one from the carrier store; a surplus goes back to it) or 'sender'
  (every 30 minutes staff send the carriers that are not theirs back to
  their home station). What has waited 15 minutes for an empty carrier
  goes by porter (10 minutes on foot).
- Urgent first on every line, or in turn.
- One line up each wing, or a lower and an upper line, each with its own
  blower; the lab on a line of its own, or on the core line.
- Wings, floors, carriers, the transfer unit, carrier speed.

7. Output

- Carriers an hour, door to door (send dock to basket), urgent carriers;
  the wait for the line.
- Samples to the lab (taken to at the lab), medicines (order to on the
  ward).
- Each line busy; stations without an empty carrier, sends that waited
  for one, by porter.
- The dispensing robot: packs an hour, busy.

With the defaults: about 260 carriers an hour, and about 60 % of them
empty: every sample and every order of medicines means an empty carrier
travels back. Door to door about 32 s, samples at the lab about 1.5 min
after they are taken, medicines on the ward about 4.5 min after the
order. The core line, which carries the store's empties as well as the
pharmacy's medicines, is the busiest at about 45 %. Staff sending
empties home now and then instead: the pharmacy runs out, medicines take
about 18 min and some 200 loads a day go by porter. The lab on the core
line: the core line 67 % busy and a carrier waits about 1.5 min for it on
average, one in ten more than 7 min.

Not modelled: air pressure and blower power, carriers that jam or come
open, the lab's analysers, maintenance of the tubes.
