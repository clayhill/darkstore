DarkStore - climbing robots
===========================

Read common.txt and acr.txt first. Implemented as RackRider.

1. Overview

The second variant of acr.txt (Exotec Skypod style). The racking is the same:
totes single deep on both sides of aisles one robot wide. But the robot has
no mast: it climbs the racking itself. It drives on the floor, into the
aisle beside the tote, grips the rack uprights and climbs straight up to the
tote's level, takes it with a telescopic arm and climbs down. A robot carries
one tote, and brings it to the station itself: there are no conveyors, the
robot is the conveyor.

2. Layout

- As acr.txt: aisles one robot wide between racks with totes single deep,
  [aisle][level][side][bay]; cross aisles in front and behind; a corridor,
  parking and chargers on the side. Aisles are one-way, neighbouring aisles
  in opposite directions.
- The racking is taller than StackPilot's shelving (climbing is fast).
- Each station is a frame at the floor's front edge at the end of a short
  queue lane. The robot drives into it and climbs to working height to
  present its tote; the operator stands in front of the frame.

3. Equipment

Robot
  - Drives at up to 4 m/s on the floor (acceleration and braking), and
    changes direction by turning its wheels, not itself.
  - Climbing: a fixed time to grip the uprights (and to let go again on the
    floor), then up or down at climbing speed.
  - While it climbs it blocks its cell of the aisle: robots behind it wait.
  - A telescopic arm takes a tote from the rack or puts it back, either
    side.
  - Battery with charging trips; climbing drains more than driving.

4. Retrieval

1. A retrieval goes to the nearest idle robot, while its station has room
   for more robots on their way.
2. The robot drives to the aisle cell beside the tote, climbs to its level,
   takes it and climbs down.
3. It queues in the station's lane, drives into the frame, climbs to working
   height and presents the tote until the operator has worked every line on
   it. If another station wants the tote meanwhile, it goes there next.
4. It puts the tote away in a free position (velocity-based, as acr.txt) and
   climbs back down.

5. Output

On top of the common output:

- Robot cycles per hour (fetch, present, put away).
- How a cycle splits into driving, climbing, the arm, presenting and waiting.
- Aisle waits, and how many robots are up the racking.

6. Parameters

These are the defaults in js/climb/config.js. A real system is larger (racking
up to 12 m, hundreds of robots); these values keep the 3D view readable.

  aisles               8, bays 14 per side
  levels               20, 0.42 m apart (8.4 m)
  robots               20, one tote each
  floor                4 m/s, 2 m/s2
  climbing             1.5 m/s, 1.2 s to grip or let go of the uprights
  arm                  3 s per take or put
  station              robot presents at 0.55 m, queue lane 3 cells
  orders               320 per hour

Not modelled yet: robots passing under a climbing robot, double-deep racking,
several robots climbing the same rack face.
