DarkStore - robots alongside pickers (collaborative AMRs)
=========================================================

Read common.txt first.

1. Overview

This one is not goods-to-person. Storage is ordinary low shelving that
people can reach, and people still walk the aisles and pick. What the robots
take over is the walking with a cart: a robot carries a few order totes from
shelf to shelf and waits beside each one until a picker comes, picks into the
right tote and confirms on the robot's screen. Pickers stay in a zone of
aisles and go from robot to robot; robots come and go. When the totes are
full the robot drives itself to a drop-off point.

It is the most common first step into warehouse automation, because the
shelving and the people stay, and robots can be added a few at a time.

2. Layout

- A location is [aisle][side][bay][level][slot]. A shelf bay is one storage
  unit in the model; it never moves.
- The floor is a grid: shelf cells, aisle cells (aisles two cells wide, so a
  robot waiting at a shelf leaves room to pass), cross-aisles at the back and
  front, station lanes, parking places and chargers.
- The robot stops on the aisle cell beside the shelf bay; the picker stands
  next to it.
- Induct & drop-off stations sit at the front. Empty order totes come off
  the station's stack and are staged there, one per released order, until a
  robot takes them; full totes go
  from there onto the takeaway conveyor to packing.

3. Equipment

- Robots: a drive unit with a small tote rack (two decks, two totes each) and
  a tablet on a mast that shows the picker what to pick. Acceleration,
  braking, turning on the spot, battery use with charging trips.
- Pickers: people walking at a set speed. They do not reserve floor cells;
  they step around robots. Per stop: scan the location, then for each line
  pick the units into the tote and confirm.
- Station operator: takes full totes off a robot and puts empty ones on, a
  fixed time per tote.

4. Picking

1. Orders are released to a station with a free staging slot and an empty
   tote, and their stock is reserved as in common.txt.
2. A robot takes up to N staged orders (its tote positions). It waits for a
   full batch unless the oldest order has waited long enough.
3. Its stops are the shelf locations of all the batch's lines, one stop per
   location. It drives them in an S-shape: aisles left to right, into the
   first from the front, out of the next one at the back, and so on.
4. At each stop it waits for a picker. Each picker takes the stop in their
   zone that has waited longest, also stops a robot is still driving to, so
   they meet it there. An idle picker helps out in another zone where robots
   are waiting.
5. After the last stop the robot queues at an induct & drop-off station. The
   operator takes the totes off onto the packing conveyor and, if orders are
   staged there, puts the next batch on. Otherwise the robot parks.

Putaway is the same tour the other way round: the goods-in operator loads
received cases onto a robot, and at each stop a picker puts the goods into
the location the IMS reserved for them.

5. Traffic

- Robots reserve cells and route with A*, as on the other robot floors. A
  robot waiting at a stop blocks its cell; others pass in the other half of
  the aisle.
- Station lanes are driven in order; idle robots wait in parking rows along
  the front and back so they keep the aisles clear.
- Pickers walk over aisle and cross-aisle cells without blocking robots.

6. Storage policy

Locations do not move, so the choice is where to pick a line from: the IMS
prefers a location already on a stop for that station, then one in an aisle
with little work waiting, then one near the stations. This spreads the work
over the picker zones.

7. Output

- Picker utilization (walking and picking), metres walked.
- How long a robot waits for a picker at a stop.
- Lines per stop, totes dropped per station.
- The robots' time split: driving, waiting for pickers, at stations,
  in traffic.

8. Parameters

- Shelving: 6 aisles, 14 bays per side, 4 levels × 2 slots per bay; a cell is
  0.75 m.
- Robots: 10, 4 order totes each, 1.2 m/s, 0.8 s to turn.
- Pickers: 6, walking 1.1 m/s, zone picking on.
- Pick: 1.5 s scan per stop, then per line 1 s + 3.2 s per unit + 1 s confirm.
- Station: 3.5 s per tote on or off; 8 orders staged per station; a robot
  leaves with a part batch after 30 s.
