DarkStore - multi-storey fulfilment building
============================================

Read common.txt and shuttle.txt first. Implemented as FloorStack (roadmap
item 37).

1. Overview

Where land is dear, a fulfilment centre goes up instead of out: storage,
picking and the dock each get a floor of their own, one above the other.
Totes and parcels then have to change floors, by spiral conveyors and
vertical lifts, and those links become the new bottleneck: they carry
everything that goes from the store to a station and back, and the way up
is long.

FloorStack is one such building with three floors:

  second floor  pick stations with put walls, packing, a control room,
                packaging supplies
  first floor   a shuttle store (as RailRunner) with a conveyor loop in
                front of its aisles
  ground floor  goods-in stations with the suppliers' trucks and pallet
                staging, the parcel sorter with roll cages, a truck per
                carrier at the dock doors, the canteen

  store → loop → spiral up → station line → pick station → spiral down →
  loop → back into the racking
  goods-in: store → loop → tote lift down → goods-in line → station →
  tote lift up → loop → racking
  packing → parcel spiral (two floors down) → sorter loop → chute →
  roll cage → truck

2. Layout

- The building's floors share their x and y (floor cells, 0.65 m); a
  floor's height is its own. Storeys: the ground floor 14 render levels,
  the first as high as the racking needs (16 with 8 levels), the second 10.
- First floor: the racking of shuttle.txt with its aisle fronts to the
  south. In front of the aisles a one-way loop: the near line runs west past
  every aisle's outfeed and infeed, on past the tote lift and the foot of the
  spiral up, round its west end and back east along the far line, past the
  tote lift's outfeed, round its east end where the spiral down lands.
- Second floor: a station line runs east from the top of the spiral up past
  every pick station (a spur in, an outfeed back onto it) to the top of the
  spiral down; a return line takes totes round again.
- Ground floor: the same for the goods-in stations, between the tote lift's
  outfeed and its infeed.
- The pick stations and the goods-in stations stand in the same row (the
  stations' front), on different floors, so the shared station layout holds
  for both; packing is beside the pick stations, as in every type.
- Openings in the slabs where spirals and lifts pass through; a staff core
  with a goods lift and stairs in the south-west corner.

3. Equipment

Store: shuttle.txt (a shuttle per level, a lift per aisle, level buffers).

Belts
  - Fixed speed (0.8 m/s), totes a pitch apart (0.72 m), accumulating; where
    two belts run into one the main line goes first, a tote waiting to join
    gets its turn after a few seconds.
  - A routing decision at each node: off onto a spur (a station, an aisle's
    infeed, a link) when it is the tote's and has room, else straight on.
Spiral conveyor
  - A belt that climbs one storey in a helix (radius 1.3 m, 1.2 m a turn),
    at the belt speed: a ride of about 46 s, holding 50 totes or so.
Tote lift (vertical reciprocating conveyor)
  - A carriage with room for 2 totes between two floors, flow-through: totes
    roll on from the north and off to the south at both stops.
  - Travel at 1.5 m/s with 1 m/s² ramps, 2.5 s for a tote to roll on or off.
  - Serves the stop whose tote has waited longest, the one it is at first.
  - In 'lift' mode the first and second floor are linked by a tote lift each
    way instead of the spirals.
Parcel spiral and sorter
  - Parcels drop two floors down the parcel spiral (1 m/s) onto a sorter
    loop that runs high (2 m) along the north wall.
  - Each carrier has a dock door with two chutes; a stainless slide drops a
    parcel into the roll cage under the chute (20 parcels a cage).
Dock
  - A loader per door: changes a full cage for an empty one (the chute is
    closed meanwhile) and pushes it into the truck, or beside the door while
    no truck is there; before a departure the cages a quarter full too.
  - A truck takes 8 cages; it leaves when full or at its carrier's departure
    time (every 45 min, the carriers in turn), the next one docks 4 minutes
    later and brings the empty cages back.
People
  - Operators walk to the canteen on their breaks (one every 40 minutes, in
    turn), from the second floor by the goods lift; a station stops until
    its operator is back.
  - The goods lift stops where it is called, opens its doors, takes its
    riders where they go (1 m/s).
  - A pallet mover takes pallets off the suppliers' trucks into the staging
    lanes and from there to the goods-in stations, which work through a
    pallet's cartons (12 receipt lines a pallet).

4. Retrieval

1. As shuttle.txt up to the aisle's lift, which rolls the tote off its
   carriage onto the aisle's outfeed, while the first floor's loop is less
   than 70 % full.
2. The tote joins the loop and turns off at the link to its station's floor:
   the spiral up for a pick station, the tote lift for goods-in. Without room
   there it goes round the loop again.
3. Upstairs (downstairs) it turns into its station's spur when the spur and
   the station's infeed have room, else it goes round the floor's loop.
4. Done at the station, it goes onto the outfeed: on to the next station
   that wants it (on that floor, or by the links), or back down to the first
   floor, where it gets its storage position and heads for that aisle's
   infeed. A tote on its way back that a station asks for meanwhile goes to
   that station instead.
5. Up to 10 totes may be on their way to a station (6 in RailRunner): the way
   from the store is long.

5. Traffic

- Belts accumulate; a full spur or link sends a tote round its floor's loop.
- The aisle lifts hold totes back while the first floor's loop is 70 % full,
  so it can always empty.
- Totes on the goods-in line wait at the tote lift's infeed for it.
- A full roll cage closes its chute; parcels for it take the carrier's other
  chute or go round the sorter.

6. Storage policy

As shuttle.txt: fast movers near the aisle fronts, in the aisle with the
least work queued (on its infeed and heading there). A tote's position is
chosen when it reaches the first floor; when its aisle's infeed is full it
goes to the next aisle along with room, or once round.

7. Output

On top of the common output and shuttle.txt's:

- Totes per hour up and down between the first and second floor, how full
  the spirals are (or how busy the lifts), totes waiting at their foot.
- The tote lift's utilization and totes per hour each way.
- Recirculation on each floor's loop.
- Parcels sorted per hour, parcels going round the sorter, cages per truck,
  trucks out and the next departure.
- A control room on the second floor with screens showing these.

8. Parameters

  building    3 floors; ground 14, first 16, second 10 render levels
  store       6 aisles, 8 levels, 20 bays (shuttle.txt otherwise)
  stations    4 pick stations (put walls of 6), 2 goods-in stations;
              10 totes in flight per station
  packing     4 benches and an auto-bagger
  orders      400 an hour
  belts       0.8 m/s, 0.72 m a tote; the loop limit 70 %
  spirals     radius 1.3 m, 1.2 m a turn
  tote lifts  1.5 m/s, 1 m/s², 2.5 s a transfer, 2 totes a carriage
  sorter      1 m/s, 0.55 m a parcel, 2 chutes a carrier, 20 parcels a cage
  trucks      8 cages, every 45 minutes per carrier, 4 min to the next one
  people      1.2 m/s walking, a break every 40 minutes in turn, 15 minutes;
              the goods lift 1 m/s

With these FloorStack picks about 830 lines an hour and keeps up with 400
orders an hour; the pick stations are about 40 % busy and wait for totes
nearly half the time, because a tote spends about 90 s on the way up. With a
tote lift each way instead of the spirals the lifts are 97 % busy, about
1200 totes an hour go round the first floor's loop because the way up is
full, and the building falls behind (about 430 lines an hour).
