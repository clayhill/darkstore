DarkStore - layer picking for beverage distribution
===================================================

Read common.txt first, but note: this type keeps its own products, orders
and pallets and does not use the shared stock, stations or packing. Of
common.txt the time model, the control room and the disruptions apply.
Implemented as LayerLift (layerlift.html, js/layer/).

1. Overview

A distribution centre for drinks: water, soft drinks, beer crates, cans,
juice and wine. Customers (supermarkets, cafés, hotels, wholesale) order
cases of a few products, often whole layers of a pallet. A gantry robot
picks whole layers at a time; what is short of a full layer is put on by
hand.

- Pick field: a row of pallet flow lanes, one product each. The front
  pallet of a lane is picked from; full pallets wait behind it and roll
  forward when it is gone.
- Gantries on an overhead runway over the lanes and the build places in
  front of them. A gantry comes down round the top layer of a lane's front
  pallet, clamps and grips it, and sets it on a customer's pallet. It also
  lifts an emptied pallet off its lane into the magazine at its end of the
  runway.
- Top-off: a person puts the odd cases on top of the last pallet of an
  order. Then the stretch wrapper, the labeller and a route truck.
- A forklift brings full pallets from the reserve rack along the north
  wall to the back of the lanes, and takes stacks of empties away.

Inspired by layer picking in general (Cimcorp, KUKA, Symbotic and others);
not any one product.

2. Layout

Positions in metres: x east, y south, z up.

- Along the north wall the reserve rack (three beam levels); south of it
  the forklift aisle.
- Lanes (default 24) 1.45 m apart, each 3 Euro pallets deep (1.2 x 0.8 m),
  sloping gently to the front.
- The runway: two rails 4.6 m up, the length of the field and the two
  magazines at its ends. Build places (default 8) along the field in front
  of the lanes, each with a dispenser of empty pallets beside it.
- South of the build places the collector, east to the top-off station,
  the wrapper, the labeller and the doors (default 2) in the south wall.

3. Equipment

- Gantry: 2 m/s along the runway (0.5 m/s²), trolley 1 m/s, head 0.8 m/s;
  gripping a layer 3.5 s (clamps in, vacuum on, a check), letting go 2.5 s.
  It travels with the head up at 2.95 m, clear of every pallet.
- Products: a layer is as many cases as fit on a Euro pallet (6 to 16), a
  supplier's pallet 3 to 8 layers.
- Top-off: 6 s a case. Wrapper 35 s, labeller 6 s, conveyors 0.4 m/s.
- Route trucks of 22 pallets; one leaves when full, or 20 minutes after
  its first pallet; the next backs up 2 minutes later.

4. Flow

An order (default 12 an hour) has 4 to 10 lines; a line is 0 to 3 whole
layers and often some cases more. Its layers are stacked heaviest first,
as many as a pallet takes (1.8 m, 1000 kg); the odd cases go on top of
the last pallet, or a pallet of their own when that is too high.

A free build place takes the next pallet; an empty pallet rolls in from
its dispenser (10 s). A gantry takes the most urgent job its runway
allows: the next layer for the pallet waiting longest, or an emptied
pallet off a lane that a pallet waits for (as urgent as that pallet). A
finished pallet rolls onto the collector; a pallet with cases stops at
the top-off station.

The forklift brings a full pallet to the lane with the fewest layers left
that has room, from a level of the reserve rack.

5. Traffic

Two gantries share the runway. A gantry reserves the stretch it will move
in (from where it is to everywhere the job takes it) before it starts,
clear of the other's stretch and of where the other stands; an idle
gantry in the way goes back to its own end. A job whose stretch would
need the other gantry beyond its end waits. Each gantry prefers jobs in
its half and uses the magazine at its end.

6. Policies

- One or two gantries; lanes, depth, build places, magazines, doors.

7. Output

- Layers and cases per hour, the share of cases picked in layers.
- Orders shipped, order to truck.
- Gantries and the top-off station busy; lanes empty; route trucks.

With the defaults: about 7 layers and 26 hand-picked cases an order, 78 %
of the cases picked in layers, order to truck about 35 minutes. One gantry
on its own manages about 75 layers an hour; a second on the same runway
adds only about half as much again (the gantries 70 % and 55 % busy at
the default load). The top-off station, at about 55 %, is the other
bottleneck to watch.

Not modelled: layers of mixed products, slip sheets, damaged layers, the
pallet store behind the reserve rack.
