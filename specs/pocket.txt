DarkStore - fashion pocket sorter
=================================

Read common.txt first, but note: this type keeps its own orders and items
(fashion, single items) and does not use the shared stock, stations or
packing. Of common.txt the time model, the control room and the
disruptions apply. Implemented as PocketFlow (pocketflow.html, js/pocket/).

1. Overview

Fashion e-commerce: many small orders (most of one or two items) and many
returns (in fashion about 40 % of what is sold comes back). Every item
travels alone in a pocket: a pouch hanging from a trolley on an overhead,
friction-driven rail (a hanging garment rides on a hanger instead). The
pocket system is a dynamic buffer between picking and packing:

- Pickers pick from ordinary shelving into totes that come to loading
  stations; returned items that can be sold again come to a returns
  loading station. An operator scans each item and puts it into an empty
  pocket held open in front of them.
- The pockets wait on storage rails until their order is complete.
- A returned item waits in its pocket as stock; a new order for the same
  article takes it from there instead of having it picked. A good share
  of returns is sold again the same day without ever going back to the
  shelving.
- A sequencer puts a batch of complete orders in exact order, and the
  pockets open one after the other over a pack station.

Inspired by pocket sorters in general (Knapp, Dematic, Vanderlande,
Savoye, Dürkopp and others); not any one product.

2. Layout

- The main loop: up the west side past the loading stations, east along
  the top past the exits of the storage rails and sequencer lines, down
  the east side, west along the bottom past their entries.
- Storage rails (default 20 of 13 m, 70 pockets each) across the loop
  like the rungs of a ladder: in from the bottom, out at the top. Each is
  first in, first out. The first 8 hold returns waiting for an order (the
  stock rails); the rest hold the pockets of orders.
- The sequencer: two banks of N lines (default 8) next to the storage
  rails, in the same ladder.
- Off the east side a feeder runs over the pack stations; each station's
  spur winds to and fro over it as a buffer for a whole sequenced batch,
  then comes down over the packing table. The last spur goes to the
  restock station, where old returns go back to the shelving.
- From the stations a return rail takes the empty pockets to the
  empty-pocket buffer, a rail winding to and fro along the west wall, and
  a distribution line feeds them to the loading stations' spurs. The rail
  comes down to chest height at a loading station.

3. Equipment

- Rail: 1.0 m/s; pockets queue 0.2 m apart. 1000 pockets.
- Loading: 6 s per item (scan, open, put in). 3 stations for picked items,
  1 for returns.
- Pickers: 9, 34 s per item including walking; a tote of 12 items, or
  part-filled after 150 s, comes to the loading station with the least to do.
- Returns desk: 3 people, 28 s per item; 75 % can be sold again.
- Pack station: the pocket opens over the table (2.2 s); the packer packs
  an order once all of it is on the table: 18 s + 4 s an item. 4 stations.

4. Flow

Order: arrives (Poisson, default 380 an hour; 1-4 items, 48/30/14/8 %).
For each item, if a return of that article waits unallocated in a pocket,
the order takes it; otherwise the item is picked. A return loaded into a
pocket is first offered to an order still waiting for that article to be
picked (the pick is cancelled), else it becomes stock.

Storage: a loaded pocket rides the loop to a storage rail. A return
waiting for an order goes on the emptiest stock rail. An order's pocket
goes behind pockets of older orders (the rail whose back pocket belongs to
the newest older order), so it is in nobody's way when its turn comes;
without one, the emptiest rail. When its rails are full a pocket goes on
the emptiest other rail.

Digging out returns: a return an order has taken is usually deep inside a
stock rail. A stock rail is swept once it holds 3 taken returns or one has
waited 5 minutes: its front pockets leave one by one, the taken ones for
the order rails, the others round the loop back to the stock rails. One
sweep for several costs far fewer moves than one each.

Ready: an order is ready once all its pockets are on the order rails.

5. Sequencing

When bank A is free, the oldest ready orders (up to N^2 pockets and 30
orders; at least 8 orders, or the oldest has waited a minute) form a
batch for the pack station with least to do. Each pocket gets its place
in the batch, 0 ... n-1.

Pass 1: the batch's pockets leave their storage rails (a pocket in front
of one goes round the loop to a storage rail again) and go into bank A,
line (place mod N).

Pass 2: once all are in bank A and bank B is free, bank A's lines are
emptied one after the other, line 0 first; each pocket goes into bank B,
line (place div N). A line goes only when the one before is empty and its
pockets are past the next line's exit, so none can get in front of them.

Out: once all are in bank B, its lines are emptied the same way, at most
as many pockets on their way to the pack station as its spur holds. Each
bank-B line now holds its pockets in order of their last digit, so the
pockets arrive in exact order 0 ... n-1: a two-pass radix sort on rails.
Bank A takes the next batch while bank B sends this one.

At the pack station the table takes the order being packed and the next
one; a pocket waits above until there is room.

6. Policies

- Storage: by order age behind older orders (above); stock rails for
  returns.
- Sweep thresholds for the stock rails: digTaken, digWait.
- Old returns: after stockAge hours (default 8) a return waiting at the
  front of its rail goes to the restock station.
- Batch size: batchOrders; the sequencer size N.

7. Output

- Orders shipped and items packed per hour, order to parcel (average,
  90th percentile), orders open.
- Items sold from returns (share), returns waiting in pockets, sent back
  to the shelving.
- Pockets in storage, empty pockets, pockets sent round again per item
  loaded (recirculation: the price of first-in-first-out rails).
- Loading and packing busy, the last 15 minutes; batches.
- The pockets by state (empty, return in stock, order incomplete, ready
  and sequencing) over time.

With the defaults the system keeps up with 380 orders an hour: about 720
items an hour into pockets, about 12 minutes from order to parcel, about
a quarter of the items sold from returns, and about two pockets
sent round for every item loaded, nearly all to dig returns out of the
stock rails.

Not modelled: the shelving and its stock (a picked item is always
there), items too large or heavy for a pocket, the garment-on-hanger
flow's own rails, rejects at loading.
