CubeFlow - how the simulation should work
=========================================

1. Time and state

- Fixed time steps of 100 ms, plus an event queue for things that happen at a
  known time. The steps need to be small enough to show travel, winch and
  picking times properly.
- Every robot runs its own state machine, with states such as IDLE,
  MOVING_TO_TARGET, DIGGING_BIN, TRANSPORTING_TO_PORT and WAITING_IN_TRAFFIC.
- The grid is a 3D array [X][Y][Z]. X and Y are positions on the top surface,
  Z is the depth of a stack (real systems go up to around 16 bins deep).

2. Parts of the system

Fleet manager (routing)
  Plans paths over the top of the grid and re-routes robots when needed, so
  they don't collide and don't end up in gridlock or deadlock.

Inventory system
  Knows which product (SKU) is in which bin and how deep each bin is. Reserves
  stock for orders and decides which bins have to be fetched, and by which
  robot.

Physics
  - acceleration and braking
  - winch speed, slower when it carries a heavy bin than when it is empty
  - battery use, with robots driving to the chargers at the edge of the grid
    on their own when they run low

3. Rules

- One robot per [X][Y] cell at any time step.
- While a robot is digging, its cell can't be passed by anyone until the winch
  is back up.
- Track how popular each product is. Fast movers should be stored near the
  top (Z=1). When a fast mover ends up deep in a stack, say at Z=10 because
  other bins were put on top of it, log the extra digging time as a burial
  penalty.

4. Output

The point of the simulator is the numbers. It should report at least:

- Throughput: bins delivered to the ports per hour, and order lines picked per
  hour (LPH).
- Robot utilization: share of time spent working, compared with idle time and
  time stuck waiting in traffic.
- Dig ratio: moves that only shift bins out of the way, divided by moves that
  deliver the bin that was asked for.
- Bottleneck alerts: highlight grid areas with heavy traffic and ports that
  keep waiting for bins.
